# V3 ship

Add-ons for the V3 ship in Kari's [Starship Expansion Project](https://github.com/Kari1407/Starship-Expansion-Project). Needs SEP and B9PartSwitch, plus Breaking Ground for the winch's pistons. Nothing in SEP's own folder is changed.

## Expendable core and fairing

**Block 3 Expendable Core** (`STMS_B3_CoreExp`) is a part of its own: SEP's core with no tiles, aerocovers or fin and leg nodes, in bare steel or (Hull switch) white. SEP models its external stringer rows only on the leeward half, where the tiles left the steel bare; a copy turned 180 degrees (`STMS_B3_Stringers`, cut from your SEP install at build time by `v3/scripts/derive_stringers.py`) closes them round the windward side. **Block 3 Payload Plate and Fairing** (`STMS_B3_FairingBase`) closes its top: a payload plate and a two-half fairing from SimpleAdjustableFairings, 0 to 4 wall segments of 2.5 m. The switch below stays on SEP's own core for existing craft.

## Expendable config for the V3 body

`SEP_26_SHIP_CORE` gains a **Recovery Hardware** switch in the VAB. Expendable removes the tiles, ablative strip, aerocovers, flap pipes and flap nodes, swaps in bare-steel masks, and drops skin temperature to 1700 K.

Mass is 23.82 t against 25 t, from SEP's own V1 ratio: its expendable body saves 1 t for 187.42 m2 of tiles and aerocovers (5.336 kg/m2), and V3 removes 220.60 m2. Only the body changes; the V3 nose and cargo section keep their tiles.

## Block 3 crew nosecones

Two new parts in the Pods tab, each a copy of SEP's Block 3 nosecone (`SEP_26_SHIP_NOSECONE`) made at load time from your SEP install. They keep SEP's model, flap nodes, RCS and Ablative switch, and add our window kit as a second model: ITS-style frames and glass with cabin lights (Light action group). Nothing of SEP's is shipped, and SEP's own nosecone is unchanged.

| Part | Windows | Crew | Mass |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Block 3 Crew Nosecone** (`STMS_B3_NoseCrew`) | The ITS big lattice window, and 28 small ones in three rows below it | 12 | SEP's + 2 t |
| **Block 3 Crew-Cargo Nosecone** (`STMS_B3_NoseCrewCargo`) | The ITS big lattice window, and one row of 8 small ones | 4 | SEP's + 1 t |

The big window is 4.2 m wide along the skin at its straight lower edge (z 2.65 in the nose's frame) and arches up to z 4.6, about 2 m. The rows' count per nose is one number, `NOSE_SMALL_COUNT` in `v3/scripts/layout.py`.

The tip of both noses is a hinged cap over a flat seat with a node for a docking port, which it shields while shut. An EVA hatch at az 0 opens on a small airlock (z 0.96 to 2.04) and lines up with the crew access arm. The crew-cargo nose's barrel is a cargo bay with SEP's clamshell doors, a floor and a ceiling node and a light. SEP's cargo section is a whole nose of its own, so nothing stacks on it.

With RasterPropMonitor or Hullcam VDS, SEP's Block 3 ship core and noses, the expendable core and both crew noses get a full set of hull cameras after the livestream views: all of them SEP's own (flaps, engine bay, payload bay), with nothing of ours added to SEP's parts; see [HLS.md](HLS.md#hull-cameras).

**Docking door** (a switch on both noses) puts the side docking bay's door in one of three places. **Side door** (the default, so saved ships keep their port) is the bay at azimuth 30 on the right; its door folds back round the hull. **Centred door** puts it on the centre line: on the crew nose under the third window row, folding up over the hull; on the crew-cargo nose in place of the EVA hatch, folding aside, because the cargo doors fill the barrel below. There the EVA hatch moves into the cargo bay, under a hatch in its ceiling, so the crew can only get out with the cargo doors open, and the crew access arm on the tower meets the docking door instead. **No door** closes the hull. Away from the side position, the four windows and the small tile patch that the side door covers come back, so the nose is symmetric. The side port's node is `port`, the centred one's `ctrport`; only the fitted door's node is active, and either is shielded while its door is shut. Toggle Docking Door works whichever door is fitted. The cabins follow the switch (with FreeIva, through its `B9PSConditionalProp`): the side door's tunnel and hatch on the commons deck, or the four windows in their place; with the centred door, a hatch housing over the port (on the crew nose's lowest deck; on the crew-cargo nose where the EVA chamber was, with a floor hatch down to the airlock in the cargo bay). Without FreeIva the cabins keep the side-door layout.

The openings are cut in SEP's own meshes at build time from your install (`v3/scripts/derive_nose.py`), and B9 gives the cut pieces SEP's textures from SEP's folder. The result is SEP's art: it stays in `build/` and `GameData/`, which git ignores.

Fit: the windows sit on the leeward bare steel only (azimuth 0, Unity -Z; the tiles face +Z), clear of the tiles with either Ablative subtype, the two small tile patches at mid-height, the cameras and the flap aerocovers. Every frame and rib is bent onto the 48 facets of SEP's shell and stands 14-24 mm proud. The surface comes from SEP's ring radii, stored as numbers in `v3/scripts/layout.py`, and `v3/scripts/check_nose.py` tests the numbers and the kits against SEP's model on every build where SEP is installed. Crew numbers: the shell holds 184 m3, so 12 kerbals get 15 m3 each; the crew-cargo cabin is the 50 m3 top deck.

## Block 3 cargo section with an awning door

**Block 3 Cargo Section (Awning Door)** (`STMS_B3_CargoAwning`, Payload tab) is a copy of SEP's Block 3 cargo section (`SEP_26_SHIP_CARGO`) made at load time from your SEP install, with the HLS awning section's bay inside. SEP's clamshell doors give way to one door, hinged across the top of SEP's opening (±45°, the leeward steel), that swings up like an awning; its outside is SEP's own door skin, tile patches included, cut at the hinge line, with the rest of that skin left as fixed hull above it. Inside are the HLS bay (floor, walls, top bulkhead, winch-boom beams on the **Boom mount** switch), its ramp over the sill and the bay lights. The nodes are the HLS cargo section's: `cargo` on the floor, `crane` by the sill for the **Crane Frame** and Cargo Winch Hoist, `winch` on the ceiling for the boom, `floor1`/`floor2` for the Cargo Floors. SEP's header tank, flaps, tiles, Ablative switch, RCS and flap cameras stay; the door is narrower than the HLS one (±52.5°), but the crane frame clears it. The model is cut by `its/scripts/derive_b3cargo.py`, which runs `build_hlscargo.py` in its Block 3 mode; the part is `its/patches/STMS_B3_CargoAwning.cfg`.

## Storage depot

**Starship Depot Tank and Nose** (`STMS_B3_Depot`) goes on the **Block 3 Expendable Core**: the tall bare-steel depot of NASA/SpaceX's configurations chart, 7.6 m longer than a normal ship. Room for 50,000 units (196 t) on top of the core's 82,000, launched empty by default. Tankers dock to SEP's hardware on the core. Reaction control, probe core and skin solar panels are built in; with CryoTanks installed it loses a tenth of what SEP's tanks do.

**Depot Nose (White)** (`STMS_HLS_Depot`), after SpaceX's render of an HLS docked to a depot: set the Expendable Core's **Hull** to White and put this nose on it. Its four bays take HLS solar arrays; blue position lights; launch it empty.

## Forward RCS

Every SEP ship nose (Block 1, 2 and 3: nosecone, cargo and Pez, expendable ones too) has a **Forward RCS** switch in the editor, off to begin with. It is on by default on our own noses and the fairing base. SEP's prototype nose has forward nozzles already. On Block 3 the edge of SEP's extended ablative strip crosses the pods: with **Ablative: Extended** the part of each pod under the edge is ablative too.

## Cargo winch

1. Attach **Cargo Winch Boom** to the `winch` node on the ceiling of `SEP_26_SHIP_CARGO`, and roll it so the rails point out of the door.
2. Attach **Cargo Winch Hoist** under the boom's crossbeam, then **Cargo Winch Platform** under the hoist.
3. Put cargo on the deck. In flight: open the doors, take the boom to -4.4, then lower the hoist toward -29.1. The sliders are hidden while a piston is Locked. Have the hoist at -3 or higher before the boom moves: below that the deck scrapes the bay floor. In action groups the names run backwards, since travel is negative: Retract lowers and runs out. At 0 the platform hangs right under the winches, with 2.4 m clear above the deck under the hoist crossbeam; the deck reaches the bay floor at about -3. Reverse it to bring the platform back, and retract fully before closing the doors.

Turn autostrut off on the winch parts and anything on the platform, or the pistons will lock up. The cables are a rigid piston under the skin, so the load doesn't swing.
